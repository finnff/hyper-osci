# Carrier PCB v1.1 — Critical Design Review Findings

**Date:** 2026-07-26 · **Method:** multi-agent adversarial review (4 independent analysts:
firmware cross-check, power-path electrical critique, minimalist/simplification advocate,
cross-document consistency audit; all major/moderate power-path findings independently
adversarially verified). **Scope:** `hw/carrier/` vs `docs/hardware/wiring.md` (breadboard
design), firmware `src/esp32-slave/`, and the current USB-only bench rig (SuperMini + PCM5102A
+ MAX4466 + pot).

**Headline verdict:** the design is *not* broadly overcomplicated — of ~27 fitted non-connector
parts, 12 are the existing breadboard design the USB-only rig simply hasn't built yet, ~10 are
the one genuinely new block (the §4 load-sharing power path), and ~5 are pennies of decoupling.
But the load-share block's paper analysis fails adversarial review in two confirmed places, and
the documented JP1 fallback recipe contains a confirmed battery-abuse bug. All fixes are
BOM-value or doc-only — nothing touches the routed layout. Recommended default: order on
schedule, ship the Aug 21 show on JP1 + the proven manual rule; the TL431 block is the
experiment, populated only if the amended §4.4 bench evening passes.

## 2. Power-path electrical review (adversarial)

**Reviewer summary:**

Adversarial review of the pcb.md §4 load-sharing power path (Q1 DMG3415U + TL431 comparator + BC557 + D1/D2 + JP1) and its design.py realization. The netlist transcription is faithful — all pinouts (Q1 G/S/D, TL431 REF/A/K, BC557 C/B/E) and diode orientations verified correct — but the circuit's margin analysis does not survive datasheet-typical numbers. Headline: the 4.54 V trip point omits TL431 REF bias current (2 µA typ/4 µA max) through the 82k top resistor, shifting the real trip to ~4.71 V typical and 4.45–5.01 V worst-case; both §4.3 margin claims are void. State-table audit: row 1 VERIFIED (5–6 mV drop); row 2 PARTIALLY REFUTED (TL431 typically does not trip at VSW=4.68 V; isolation is D1-subthreshold only at Vgs≈−0.10 V, though CC/CV termination still functions); row 3 verified with corner caveats; row 4 REFUTED in the realistic case — a laptop port sagging to 4.4–4.6 V at the connector (routine with thin cables) leaves Q1 FULLY enhanced and back-feeds the cell at the port's current limit (1.5–3 A, no CC/CV, no termination; only backstop is DW01's 4.3 V overvoltage cut, tens of minutes away mid-SoC; D1 is inert since the charger VBUS is 0 V; missing hysteresis makes it a sticky latch, not chatter; and a stiff bench supply hides the failure, so §4.4 as written would green-light it); rows 5 and 7 verified; row 6 verified arithmetically but ~40% optimistic (D2 reverse leakage). Second independent major: the JP1 escape-hatch instructions leave D2 fitted, which with JP1 bridged connects the raw charger input to B+ at ~4.6 V unregulated — the committed §8.2 fallback path ships a battery-abuse bug unless one doc sentence is added. Pre-order fixes are all BOM-value/doc-only (no layout change): R7/R8 → 8.2k/10k 1%, TL431→TL431A, R9→1k, D1→BAT85/BAT54 Schottky (makes charging-state isolation hard-off at Vgs≈0…+0.1 V), delete the §4.4 75k-retune advice (it collides with a 4.24 V full cell), amend §4.4 to test state 4 with a deliberately sagged 4.4–4.6 V source, and add 'unfit D2' to the JP1 instructions. Judgment on alternatives: the absolute-threshold-on-the-load-node concept is structurally unable to distinguish saggy USB from a full battery (usable band ~200–300 mV, mostly eaten by tolerances), so this block is the weakest option on the table; for 4 hand-built units ordering in ~6 days with the mandated breadboard verification still not done, ship the show on JP1 + the proven operational rule (or the no-new-parts middle path: depopulate the TL431 branch, keep Q1/R6/D1/D2 for charger-side-only load sharing, retaining the switch-off rule solely for bench flashing), and put a real ideal-diode part (LTC4412, or a BQ24074/MCP73871 breakout) in a post-show revision. Order the PCB on schedule — it carries every topology — but invert the default: the TL431 block is the experiment, not the plan of record.

### [MAJOR] TL431 trip point is mis-derived: REF bias current through the 82k source impedance shifts it +164 mV typ / +330 mV max; real trip is ~4.71 V typ, 4.45–5.01 V worst-case — the doc's 4.54 V and both §4.3 margin claims are wrong — **verification: CONFIRMED**

The doc computes trip = 2.495 × 182/100 = 4.541 V and stops. TL431 REF input bias current (2 µA typ, 4 µA max, 25 °C) flows through R7 and adds I_ref × R7 to the trip: V_trip = Vref(1+R7/R8) + I_ref·R7. Numbers: typ = 4.541 + 2 µA × 82k = 4.705 V; max-bias = 4.869 V. Full corner stack with a plain-grade TL431 (BOM says 'any brand' ⇒ ±2 % Vref = 2.445–2.545 V) and 1 % resistors: low corner ≈ 2.445 × 1.804 + 0.5 µA × 81.2k ≈ 4.45 V; high corner ≈ 2.545 × 1.837 + 4 µA × 82.8k ≈ 5.01 V. Consequences: (1) §4.3 state-4 margin 'trip 4.54 vs USB ≥ 4.75 → 0.21 V' is actually +0.05 V typical and −0.26 V worst case — the TL431 is NOT guaranteed to trip even from a spec-perfect 4.75 V source, and 4.75 V is the source-side spec; a 28 AWG cable at 300–500 mA drops another 100–250 mV, so 4.55–4.65 V at the SuperMini connector is routine. (2) In state 2, VSW = 5.0 − Vf(D2@150 mA ≈ 0.32) = 4.68 V < 4.71 V typ trip ⇒ the TL431 typically does not trip while charging either; isolation rests on D1 alone (see separate finding). Battery-side margin survives: low corner 4.45 V vs charger CV max 4.2 V + 1 % = 4.24 V ⇒ 0.21 V, OK. Fix (BOM-value-only, zero layout cost, do before ordering): drop divider impedance ~10× keeping the ratio — R7 = 8.2k, R8 = 10k, 1 % (bias error 16 mV typ/33 mV max; window with TL431A ±1 % ≈ 4.46–4.66 V) and specify TL431A grade. Cost: divider drain rises 23 µA → ~230 µA, which cuts the §4.3 row-6 storage figure from '~5 years' to ~10–12 months — acceptable for show units, but restate it. Middle option 27k/33k: 54 mV typ bias error, ~70 µA. Note the §4.4 item-5 bench sweep would have caught this — it has not been run.

### [MAJOR] State 4 with a sagging SuperMini USB (laptop port 4.4–4.5 V loaded): Q1 stays FULLY enhanced and the cell is back-fed at the port's current limit (1.5–3 A, no CC/CV, no termination); D1 is inert; failure is silent and invisible to a stiff-supply bench test — **verification: CONFIRMED**

This edge is real even at the doc's believed 4.54 V trip, and the bias-current shift (previous finding) widens it to healthy sources. Mechanism: SuperMini VBUS↔5V tie (meter-confirmed, measurements.md item 5) puts 4.4–4.5 V on VLOAD→SW1→VSW. Below trip ⇒ TL431 off ⇒ GATE = 0 V via R6 ⇒ Vgs = −4.4 V ⇒ Q1 fully on at ~35 mΩ — this is not body-diode-limited back-feed, it is a dead short from USB to the cell. Path: port → cable → 5V pin → SW1 (~30–50 mΩ) → Q1 (35 mΩ) → OUT+/B+ (same copper) → cell; return via FS8205 (~50 mΩ) and cell IR (~80–150 mΩ). Loop ≈ 0.25–0.4 Ω. Cell at 3.8 V, port at 4.45 V ⇒ demanded I = 0.65/0.3 ≈ 2.2 A ⇒ the port's limiter sets the current: ~0.5 A (legacy), 1.5 A (BC1.2), up to 3 A (Type-C 5V/3A) = 0.75–1.5C uncontrolled CC charge into the LP103454. Limits: ONLY the USB source's current limit and, eventually, DW01 overcharge at ~4.3 V cell — which for a mid-SoC cell is tens of minutes to hours away. DW01 has no charge-overcurrent protection (its 150 mV CS threshold is discharge-direction), so it does not intervene before 4.3 V. D1 does not help: its anode is VBUS_CHG = 0 V (charger unplugged), so it is reverse-biased/inert — confirmed; the only component that would cover state 4 is D3 (VLOAD→GATE), which §4.1 correctly rejects because it costs the body-diode drop in battery mode. Nasty property: a bench 5.0 V supply passes state 4 cleanly (5.0 > 4.71 typ trip), so §4.4 item 3 as written will green-light the circuit; it fails specifically in its real use case — flashing from a laptop through a thin cable, switch left ON. There is no visible symptom (board runs fine; the cell silently takes amps). If the block is kept, §4.4 item 3 MUST be run from a source deliberately sagged to 4.4–4.6 V (bench supply + 0.5 Ω series), not from a stiff port.

*Refs: docs/hardware/pcb.md:205 (state 4), pcb.md:210-212, docs/hardware/measurements.md:45-47,84, hw/carrier/tools/design.py:86-87,103-104*

> **Independent verifier (CONFIRMED):** Every load-bearing assertion checks out against the netlist (design.py), the doc (pcb.md §4), the bench measurements (measurements.md), and datasheet-typical values; my strongest counter-attacks failed and one of them showed the defect is structurally worse than claimed.

(1) Topology confirmed: SW1 ties VSW to VLOAD (design.py SW1 {1:VSW,2:VLOAD}); the SuperMini 5V pin IS USB VBUS (measurements.md item 5, meter-confirmed); Q1 is G=GATE, S=VSW, D=VBAT_OUT, and VBAT_OUT is literally the cell + terminal (B+↔OUT+ measured 0 Ω). So a laptop port loaded to 4.4–4.5 V puts 4.4–4.5 V on VSW.

(2) TL431 stays hard off — I tried to refute via the TL431's soft transition and it made things worse: at VSW=4.45 V, V_REF = 4.45/1.82 − I_REF×(82k||100k) ≈ 2.445 − 0.090 = 2.355 V, i.e. 140 mV below the 2.495 V threshold (the 2 µA typ REF bias current pulls REF DOWN, deepening cutoff). Off-state cathode current ≤1 µA through R9 develops ≤0.1 V across R12 (100k) — far below BC557 V_EB(on) ≈ 0.6 V, so Q2 is off. GATE sits at 0 V via R6 (only nA-scale leakages: 1N4148 reverse ≤25 nA, Q2 I_CBO, Q1 I_GSS). Vgs = −4.45 V ⇒ DMG3415U fully enhanced: Rds(on) 30 mΩ typ / 42 mΩ max at Vgs=−4.5 V. Channel conduction is bidirectional — this is NOT body-diode back-feed (body diode anode=D is reverse-biased anyway); it is a low-ohmic channel from USB to the cell. Confirmed.

(3) D1 is inert — I tried to refute via SS34 reverse leakage lifting VBUS_CHG: SS34 leakage at 4.5 V reverse is tens of µA into the unpowered TP4056 IN+ node (charger IC input + cap), which cannot lift VBUS_CHG anywhere near GATE-rescue territory; even a hypothetical 2 V float would leave Vgs ≈ −3 V and Q1 still fully on. Confirmed.

(4) DW01 non-intervention confirmed: DW01A has overcharge (4.30±0.05 V, ~100 ms delay), overdischarge (2.4 V), and discharge-direction overcurrent (V_CS +150 mV) / short (1.35 V) only. The negative-CS threshold (~−50 mV) is charger DETECTION for releasing overdischarge latch, not a protection. There is no charge-overcurrent function. FS8205 conducts the charge direction at ~50–80 mΩ (2×25–40 mΩ in series). Time to 4.3 V from 3.8 V OCV at 1.5 A into the 2000 mAh LP103454 ≈ 35–50 min; at 0.5 A ≈ 2–3 h. "Tens of minutes to hours" confirmed.

(5) The strongest attack BACKFIRED and strengthens the claim: I checked whether the back-feed equilibrium is self-consistent. It is, and it reveals the trip is unreachable: with Q1 on, the cell clamps VSW = V_OCV + I×R_branch where R_branch (Q1 + FS8205 + JST/traces + cell DC-IR) ≈ 0.16–0.27 Ω. To reach even the ideal 4.54 V trip with a 3.8 V cell requires ≈3.0–3.7 A in the cell branch; to reach the bias-corrected typ trip of ≈4.70 V requires ≈4.5 A — beyond any USB port (legacy 0.5 A, BC1.2 1.5 A, Type-C 3–3.3 A). So the TL431 term cannot win state 4 from the Q1-on initial condition at mid SoC AT ALL — the sag scenario is merely the most obvious member of a structural failure class. The doc's state-4 row commits a static error: it evaluates the trip at the unloaded 5.0 V while the plug-in initial condition (battery mode) has Q1 on and the node clamped. The equilibrium is bistable with cell-clamp hysteresis; the on-state is the one you start in. Port OCP hiccup/latch modes don't make it visible either — the board rides through on the battery via the on FET, giving pulsed back-feed with zero user-visible symptom.

(6) The bench-green-light property is real but its mechanism is subtler than the claim states: §4.4 item 3's own series ammeter (DMM shunt + leads ≈ 0.1–0.25 Ω) roughly doubles R_branch, cutting the trip current to ≈2–3 A, which a stiff bench source CAN deliver transiently — so the test trips Q1 off and reads µA, then removing the meter restores the latched-on fault in the field. The instrument itself helps the circuit pass. (Caveat: in some parameter corners — bias-shifted trip ≈4.70 V, fresh cell, low-R meter — even a stiff 3 A source stays latched on and the bench test would visibly fail; the pass/fail is a non-reproducible coin flip over TL431 I_REF, resistor tolerance, SoC, and lead resistance, which is arguably worse than a deterministic false pass.)

(7) The bias-current "previous finding" verifies: at trip, I(R7) = 2.495/100k + I_REF, so V_trip = 4.541 + I_REF×82k = 4.70 V typ (2 µA), 4.87 V at I_REF max (4 µA); adding Vref ±2% and 1% divider tolerance the worst corner reaches ≈5.0 V — above a healthy port. Also confirmed: D3 (BAT85, VLOAD→GATE per design.py deviation 2) is the only fitted-able part that covers state 4, and §4.1's rejection reasoning (battery-mode body-diode drop) is sound; the doc's own §4.3 margin math assumes USB ≥4.75 V, but USB 2.0 itself allows 4.40 V at the device end, so 4.4–4.5 V from a laptop through a thin cable is in-spec, not exotic.

Minor overstatements that do not change the verdict: the claim's 0.25–0.4 Ω loop excludes the cable it invokes (realistic total ≈0.35–0.75 Ω incl. cable) — immaterial since the port limiter, not loop R, sets the current in every realistic case; and "a bench 5.0 V supply passes cleanly because 5.0 > 4.71" repeats the doc's static error — it passes because of transient stiffness plus the ammeter's added resistance, not because of the open-circuit voltage comparison. Additional abuse the claim missed: the 1.5–3 A back-feed also flows through SW1 (1 A rated) and the JST-PH (2 A rated), and Q1 at 3 A dissipates ≈0.3–0.4 W in SOT-23 (~+100 °C rise at θJA≈300 K/W). The proposed fix (run §4.4 item 3 from a source deliberately sagged to 4.4–4.6 V) is necessary but per (5) not sufficient — item 3 should also be run with a milliohm-class shunt instead of a DMM ammeter, and the pass criterion must hold across cell SoC 3.6–4.0 V.

### [MAJOR] JP1 escape hatch as documented leaves D2 fitted: bridging JP1 turns the charger input into a direct, unregulated ~4.6 V source onto the cell, bypassing the TP4056 entirely — **verification: CONFIRMED**

§4.4 says: bridge JP1 (VBAT_OUT→VSW), 'leave Q1/U1/Q2/D1 unfitted' — D2 is not in the unfit list, and it is a default-fit BOM item soldered long before any fallback decision. With JP1 bridged and D2 fitted, plugging the charger gives: VBUS_CHG (5 V, before the CC/CV controller) → D2 → VSW → JP1 → VBAT_OUT = OUT+ = B+ (same copper, measured) → cell. The cell is held at ~5.0 − Vf(D2) ≈ 4.6 V with no current control: cell at 3.8 V ⇒ ~0.85 V across ~0.3 Ω ⇒ ~2 A demanded, folding the mandated 2 A wall charger to its limit. The TP4056 sees B+ ≈ 4.6 V and terminates immediately, so the module's own LED even reports 'charged' while D2 force-feeds the cell. Backstop is again only DW01 OV at 4.3 V (which then opens the low-side FET, separating system GND from B−). This is exactly the §8.2 committed fallback path ('if the §4.4 test slips past Jul 30, order anyway with JP1 as the committed fallback'), so the probability of this configuration existing is high under the current schedule. Fix: one sentence in §4.4 — 'when bridging JP1, also unfit (or clip) D2 and D1'; with D2 removed, the JP1 topology is exactly the proven breadboard chain and the old charging rules apply.

### [MODERATE] No hysteresis: near the trip the circuit is bistable and with a soft (battery-loaded, sagging) source the stable state is back-feed latch-up; hysteresis is not a one-resistor TL431 fix — **verification: CONFIRMED**

For completeness, the interlocking bias checks pass: TL431 off-state cathode leakage (≤1 µA) through R9 develops ≤0.1 V across R12 — Q2 stays off; Q2 saturation margin is huge; Q1 gate needs no protection (worst |Vgs| ≈ 5.0 V transient vs ±8 V abs max, R6 bleeds the gate).

### [MINOR] State-6 storage figure (45 µA, '~5 years') ignores D2 reverse leakage into the TP4056 IN+ node and module quiescent; realistic is ~60–75 µA at 25 °C (~3 years) and hundreds of µA warm

In storage/battery mode D2 (SS34) is reverse-biased at VBAT (VSW 3.0–4.2 V on cathode, floating VBUS_CHG on anode) and leaks battery current into the TP4056 IN+ node (= charger-chip VCC + LED networks), which sinks it to GND. SS34 IR spec is 0.5 mA max at 40 V/25 °C; scaled to 4 V expect ~5–25 µA at 25 °C, roughly ×10 per 40 °C — 100–500 µA at a 60 °C summer venue/enclosure. Add DW01 (~3 µA) + TP4056 standby (~2–5 µA, power-budget.md counts these) and the honest 25 °C total is ~60–75 µA ⇒ ~3 years, not 5 — still completely irrelevant against LiPo self-discharge, but the doc quotes 45 µA/5 years with confidence it hasn't earned. Related nit: the SS34 (3 A) is oversized for a ≤0.5 A path — an SS14 would halve the leakage — but the C1-inrush argument for a big junction is defensible: plugging the charger with SW1 ON steps VSW ~0.95 V into C1 220 µF ⇒ ~4 A for <100 µs, trivial against SS34 IFSM = 100 A (also fine for SS14's 25 A, for the record). Verified while here: row 1 (5–6 mV drop at 125 mA with Rds 42 mΩ max, Vgs −3.0…−4.2 V) is correct; row 7's race mechanism is sound (D1 slews Ciss ≈ 750 pF in ns–µs, Vgs never exceeds ~−0.2 V during the VBUS ramp, residual cell blip is µC-scale) — D1's other unstated job is decoupling the gate from the TP4056 module's ~10 µF input cap, without which gate turn-off after unplug would take R6 × 10 µF ≈ 1 s of body-diode brownout.

## 3. Component-by-component necessity verdicts ("could we delete this?")

Verdict scale: **essential** / **cheap-insurance** / **debatable** / **deletable**, judged for
4 hand-built battery-powered art units, not generic best practice.

### Q1,R6 — **debatable** (charging-related: yes)

**Function:** P-FET switch (plus its gate pull-down) that connects the battery to the load with ~5 mV drop and auto-disconnects it whenever any USB is present — the core of the load-share block that exists ONLY to delete the breadboard rule 'never have the switch ON while any USB is plugged'. NEW on the PCB; exists only because a battery exists.

**If deleted:** Nothing breaks electrically — bridge JP1 and you have the exact proven breadboard topology. What you get back is the manual rule for the life of the units: one forgotten switch-flip while flashing pushes raw USB current into the LiPo past the charger's CC/CV (DW01 stops shorts, not back-feed overcharge), and charging with the switch ON can stop the TP4056 from ever terminating, parking the cell at 4.2 V under load. With 4 units and dozens of pre-show flash/charge plug-ins, one mistake is realistic.

### D1 — **essential** (charging-related: yes)

**Function:** 1N4148 from charger-USB VBUS to the FET gate: slams Q1 off in nanoseconds when a charger is plugged, closing the race before the TL431 reacts, and covers the sagging-charger case (4.75 V USB minus D2 drop lands below the 4.54 V trip) where the TL431 alone would fail.

**If deleted:** (Essential only if the block is populated.) Every charger plug-in sends a current blip into the cell during the µs–ms before the TL431 trips, and a weak charger can leave Q1 partially on so the battery and charger fight continuously — exactly the failure states 2 and 7 in pcb.md §4.3 were designed against. It is one 2-cent diode.

### D2 — **essential** (charging-related: yes)

**Function:** SS34 Schottky that feeds the load from the charger's USB while Q1 has the battery disconnected — it is what lets a unit keep running while it charges.

**If deleted:** (Within the block.) Plug a charger in with SW1 ON and the unit goes instantly dead: Q1 turns off, and with D2 gone there is no path from any source to the load. Run-while-charging (state 2) becomes impossible, which was half the point of the block.

### U1,R7,R8,Q2,R9,R12 — **debatable** (charging-related: yes)

**Function:** TL431 comparator arm that infers 'a USB is plugged into the SuperMini' by detecting VLOAD > 4.54 V — needed only because the SuperMini's VBUS is hard-wired to its 5V pin (meter-confirmed 2026-07-18), so the DESIGN §9 'diode-OR from VBUS' cannot be built directly for that port.

**If deleted:** Charging via the TP4056 port stays fully automatic (D1/D2 cover it), but flashing over the SuperMini USB with SW1 ON back-feeds the cell again — you keep a half-rule, 'switch OFF when flashing', which only bites during development, not at the show. This is the most honest deletion candidate in the block: 6 parts to erase one dev-time habit. But deleting it from the LAYOUT now costs re-route/re-DRC days before Aug 1; simply not populating it later costs nothing.

### D3 — **deletable** (charging-related: yes)

**Function:** DNP footprint for the naive VLOAD-to-gate OR diode, kept purely so you can demonstrate to yourself on real hardware why that topology fails (§4.1).

**If deleted:** Nothing — no part is fitted, it is empty board area. Deleting it saves zero BOM and zero assembly time; it only removes a teaching aid.

### JP1 — **essential** (charging-related: yes)

**Function:** 0-ohm/solder-jumper bridge from VBAT_OUT to VSW: the escape hatch that turns the whole load-share block into a populate-time OPTION instead of a schedule risk — bridge it, leave Q1/Q2/U1/D1 off, and the board is the proven breadboard topology.

**If deleted:** The power path becomes all-or-nothing: if the §4.4 bench test fails or slips past Jul 30, the PCB order waits on an unproven circuit 1 day before the deadline. JP1 is the single cheapest risk-removal on the board (a bare pad pair) and the reason pcb.md can say 'order anyway'.

### C1 — **essential** (charging-related: no)

**Function:** 220 µF bulk reservoir on VLOAD that sources the first ~50–100 µs of each 300+ mA WiFi TX burst before the cell chemistry and lead inductance catch up. ALREADY IN THE BREADBOARD DESIGN (wiring.md wire P8) — not new.

**If deleted:** Power-budget §4.3: a sub-10 µs 300 mA step across the 0.2–1 Ω battery loop dips the LDO input 60–335 mV exactly when headroom is smallest. Above ~3.7 V cell you would likely get away with it; below ~3.5 V the SuperMini brownout-resets during WiFi connect/TX — i.e. units start random-rebooting in the last third of a show run, which reads as flaky firmware and is miserable to debug on 4 units at a venue.

### C2 — **cheap-insurance** (charging-related: no)

**Function:** 100 nF ceramic companion to C1 on VLOAD, covering the electrolytic's inductance at MHz frequencies. New on the PCB.

**If deleted:** Probably nothing observable — the SuperMini module carries its own ceramics at the LDO. It is 2 cents and standard practice; deleting it saves nothing measurable and forgoes free margin. Skip at populate time if you enjoy the gesture.

### R1,R2,C3 — **essential** (charging-related: no)

**Function:** 100k/100k divider halving battery voltage into GPIO1 plus the 100 nF that lets the ADC actually sample a 50 kΩ source; feeds the firmware's entire low-battery ladder (warn 3.45 V / WiFi-off 3.30 V / sleep 3.05 V in config.h). ALREADY IN THE BREADBOARD DESIGN (wiring.md S1–S3). Battery telemetry, not charging.

**If deleted:** GPIO1 floats, vbat_mv is garbage, and every protection threshold in config.h is dead code: no low-battery LED warning, no WiFi-off save, no protective sleep. A unit dies mid-set with zero warning, then the cell rides down to the DW01's 2.4 V fire-fuse — permanent capacity damage per power-budget §5. Delete just C3 and the readings come out noisy/hundreds of mV low (the ADC's sample-and-hold cannot charge through 50 kΩ in one multiplexed slot), so thresholds false-trigger. Deleting also requires firmware surgery — this is not an optional part, it is half the product's battery management.

### R3 — **essential** (charging-related: no)

**Function:** 10 k pull-up holding ESP32-C3 strapping pin GPIO2 high at reset (the socket pin would otherwise float). ALREADY IN THE BREADBOARD DESIGN (wiring.md U10).

**If deleted:** Boot lottery: GPIO2 must read 1 during SPI boot and a floating pin is genuinely undefined — boards typically work on the bench for weeks, then one unit intermittently refuses to boot at the venue (temperature/humidity-dependent). One resistor versus a non-reproducible show-day failure; Espressif's own hardware guidelines mandate it.

### R4,D4 — **essential** (charging-related: no)

**Function:** Green NET LED + its 2.2 k series resistor: the only at-a-glance indicator of WiFi/stream state (1 Hz = connecting, solid = streaming, 5 Hz = fallback, per DESIGN §7). ALREADY IN THE BREADBOARD DESIGN (wiring.md U6/U7); firmware drives it.

**If deleted:** During show setup you cannot tell which of 4 units is actually on the stream without interpreting scope patterns; network debugging at the venue becomes guesswork. Deleting R4 but keeping the LED would put an LED straight across a GPIO — do not. 0.57 mA when lit, negligible.

### R5,D5 — **debatable** (charging-related: no)

**Function:** Amber MODE LED + series resistor showing LOCAL/NETWORK/HYBRID state. ALREADY IN THE BREADBOARD DESIGN (wiring.md U8/U9); firmware drives it.

**If deleted:** You infer mode from unit behavior instead — genuinely survivable, since low-battery signaling lives on the onboard blue LED. But it is already wired in firmware, docs, and the routed layout, so deleting it is negative work that saves 2 parts and ~0.3 mA. Keep it.

### R10,R11 — **debatable** (charging-related: no)

**Function:** 100 Ω series resistors between DAC outputs and the RCA pads, protecting against shorted/hot-plugged scope leads — except the module was measured (2026-07-18) to already carry ~470 Ω in line, so pcb.md already plans to fit 0 Ω unless the Phase-4 ramp says otherwise. New on the PCB.

**If deleted:** You cannot fully delete them — the footprint must be bridged for signal to pass, so the 'part' exists either way; the only question is 100 Ω vs 0 Ω. If the module 470 Ω is confirmed in-line, 0 Ω is correct and the risk is nil; the footprint costs nothing and preserves the option. This is already the least-commitment version of itself.

### C4 — **cheap-insurance** (charging-related: no)

**Function:** 100 nF supply decoupling at the mic pigtail header — the MAX4466 is a 25–125x gain amplifier on a 10 cm unshielded lead sharing an enclosure with a WiFi antenna, the classic RF-buzz victim. New on the PCB.

**If deleted:** Elevated odds of WiFi TX buzz riding the mic supply into LOCAL-mode patterns (visible hash on the scope every TX burst). The mic module has its own onboard decoupling, so it may well be fine without — but this is the textbook 2-cent fix for a failure mode you would only discover at the show with the PA and 4 radios running.

### C5 — **cheap-insurance** (charging-related: no)

**Function:** 100 nF decoupling near the DAC's VIN socket. New on the PCB.

**If deleted:** Almost certainly nothing — the GY-PCM5102A carries its own XC6206 LDO with local caps, so the module self-decouples. This is the most honestly deletable fitted part on the board. It costs 2 cents and is already routed, so deleting it from the layout is more work than soldering it.

### SW1 — **essential** (charging-related: no)

**Function:** 1 A-rated power slide switch between VSW and the load. ALREADY IN THE BREADBOARD DESIGN (wiring.md P3/P4). Power-path adjacent but exists regardless of the charging scheme.

**If deleted:** The only off-switch becomes unplugging the JST-PH battery connector — rated for on the order of 30 mating cycles, so daily on/off wears it out within the rehearsal period, and you cannot quickly kill a misbehaving unit on stage. The 1 A rating (not the cheaper 0.3 A part) matters because WiFi TX peaks ~0.35 A.

### SW2 — **essential** (charging-related: no)

**Function:** 6x6 mm MODE button on GPIO7 — mode cycling and hold-at-boot-for-LOCAL, per firmware/UX spec. ALREADY IN THE BREADBOARD DESIGN (wiring.md U4/U5). No external pull-up or debounce parts — firmware does both.

**If deleted:** The only remaining mode control is the onboard BOOT button, sealed inside the 3D-printed enclosure. Note this input is already maximally simple: zero support components.

### RV1 — **essential** (charging-related: no)

**Function:** 10 k pot on GPIO3 setting the LOCAL-mode Y-filter cutoff (20–300 Hz) — the live performance control carried over from the proven old sigma-delta units. ALREADY IN THE BREADBOARD DESIGN (wiring.md U1–U3); it is on your current test rig.

**If deleted:** LOCAL mode loses its expressive control and the units regress below the old hardware they replace. This is part of the art, not the electronics.

### TP1-TP5 — **cheap-insurance** (charging-related: yes)

**Function:** Five bare 1.5 mm pads on VBUS_CHG, VSW, GATE, 3V3, VBAT_SENSE — probe landings for exactly the nodes you will be metering during power-path bring-up, which otherwise hide under socketed modules.

**If deleted:** Zero BOM savings (no parts exist), zero assembly savings. Cost of deleting: when Q1 misbehaves you probe SOT-23 legs under a plugged-in TP4056 module instead of touching a pad. Three of the five exist purely for the charging block; TP4/TP5 serve general bring-up.

### System-level alternatives considered

- **Bridge JP1, do not populate the load-share block (keep the manual switch rule)** — This is the design's own built-in simplification and it costs ZERO rework: the PCB order goes out unchanged on schedule, and at assembly you solder one bridge and omit 10 parts (Q1,Q2,U1,D1,D2,R6-R9,R12, ~0.50 EUR/board). You get the exact proven breadboard topology. Price: the rule 'switch OFF before plugging ANY USB' becomes permanent operating procedure for hand-built art units that strangers may help wrangle at a show, one lapse back-feeds a bare LiPo, and you lose run-while-charging. Best move if the one-evening bench test (pcb.md 4.4) fails or does not happen by Jul 30.

- **Swap to a dev board with integrated LiPo charging (XIAO ESP32-C3, FireBeetle 2 C3 class)** — Would delete the TP4056 module, its custom-pitch sockets, AND the entire load-share block in one stroke — architecturally the cleanest answer. But it abandons 4 owned SuperMinis, invalidates the meter-confirmed VBUS finding, changes the pin map (config.h is law, so firmware + docs churn), and requires a new caliper session plus a full re-layout of a board that is already routed and DRC-clean — a week-plus of work plus parts shipping against a 6-day order deadline. Onboard chargers also charge at 100-500 mA (4-20 h on the 2000 mAh cell). Right idea for v2, not survivable for Aug 1.

- **External USB power bank per unit instead of bare LiPo + TP4056** — Deletes the battery, charger module, load-share block, sense divider, and most of the firmware power ladder — the biggest possible simplification. Fatal flaw: most power banks auto-shut-off below ~50-100 mA load, and LOCAL mode draws ~40-60 mA, so a unit that goes quiet can be killed BY ITS OWN BATTERY mid-show; you would be qualifying 4 specific banks against a behavior their datasheets do not specify. Also 4 decent banks cost more than the entire battery BOM, the enclosure grows, vbat telemetry dies, and it is still a full re-layout in 6 days.

- **Populate only the charger half of the block (Q1,D1,D2,R6; omit the TL431 arm U1,Q2,R7,R8,R9,R12)** — Charging becomes fully automatic while flashing keeps a 'switch OFF' rule — a defensible middle since the TL431 arm only protects a development-time scenario. But as a LAYOUT change it means re-routing the power quadrant this week to save ~0.10 EUR, and as a populate-time choice it is strictly dominated by JP1 (which needs no analysis at all). Worth knowing about, not worth doing.

### Bottom line

No — the design is not overcomplicated, but your instinct found the one block worth interrogating, and the design already contains its own answer. The accounting: of ~27 fitted non-connector parts, 12 (R1-R5, C1, C3, D4, D5, SW1, SW2, RV1) are the EXISTING breadboard design per wiring.md that your USB-only test rig simply hasn't built yet — mostly the battery subsystem (divider, bulk cap, switch) plus the UI you already planned (LEDs, button, pot). Ten more (Q1, Q2, U1, D1, D2, R6-R9, R12) are a single new battery/charging block — the load-share path — whose only job is to delete the human-error rule 'switch OFF before plugging any USB', and it ships with a zero-cost bypass (JP1) that turns it into a populate-time option. Only ~5 parts are genuine PCB gold-plating (C2, C4, C5 at 100 nF, R10/R11 which will likely be 0-ohm bridges), totaling maybe 10 cents. So: not 'all charging circuit' — about 10 parts are, ~15 exist because a battery exists at all, and the rest are the product's controls. Your test rig looks simple because it has no battery; the delta between it and the PCB IS the portable product. What I would actually change 6 days before the order: nothing in the layout — it is routed and DRC-clean, and deleting footprints now costs re-route/re-DRC days to save under 1 EUR/board. Order as-is by Aug 1. Then let the one-evening bench test (pcb.md 4.4) decide the load-share block's fate: pass, populate it; fail or no time, bridge JP1 and stuff 10 fewer parts — the PCB is identical either way. At populate time you may honestly skip C2 and C5 and fit R10/R11 as 0-ohm. The parts I would defend against any simplification pressure: R1/R2/C3 (low-battery ladder), C1 (WiFi brownout), and R3 (boot strap) — each is one or three cheap parts standing between you and a unit that dies silently or refuses to boot mid-show.
